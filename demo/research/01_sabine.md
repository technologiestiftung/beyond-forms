## Biography (Short)

Sabine is a single woman (50–65) living in Berlin who is reliant on long-term subsistence benefits due to a permanent full disability. She lives in a two-room apartment and has to manage on very limited means; she has no assets. Complex forms and lengthy processes quickly overwhelm her — especially since she needs cognitive support with difficult issues, and stress further hampers her concentration. It is typical for her not to handle applications "simply on her own digitally," but rather to proceed step by step in a consulting setting on the computer with a professional. Her strongest desire is for a process that is calming, provides clear guidance, explains documents early, and avoids mistakes — so that she does not get lost in additional demands and uncertainties.

### Income, Needs, and Financial Situation

- Sabine has paid into the German pension insurance for over 12 years.
- No assets.
- She receives housing benefit until the application for basic security.
- Needs (excluding housing costs, roughly): approx. €1,120 gross need; net entitlement approx. €860–890.
- Her livelihood is not secured.

### Living Situation & Fixed Costs

- Sabine lives in Berlin, Mitte district.
- 2-room apartment.

- Housing costs typically range from approx. €400–500, often higher in more expensive districts (Mitte, Neukölln, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf).

### Digital Competence & Usage Context

- Sabine has cognitive limitations and needs support with complex issues.
- Extensive applications frighten her ("no less than 80 pages if everything is included").
- Even younger clients with disabilities are often overwhelmed by apps; a PC in a counseling/office context is preferred over primarily using a smartphone.
- Supported self-service: Sabine usually uses the tool in the presence of a professional (e.g., Caritas, hospital, social welfare office) at a PC/terminal – not alone at home.
- Co-use is a core case: The tool should support "filling out together" (Sabine + social worker) without Sabine losing control.
- Sensitive to long, complex forms (risk of abandonment).

### Reasons, Fears, and Goals

- Sabine wants to apply for basic security because her livelihood is not secured.
- She is insecure and afraid of making mistakes when filling out applications/forms.
- She wishes for support/counseling that "takes her by the hand" while she fills it out herself.
- She wants to make fewer mistakes and thereby avoid creating additional work for administrative staff.
- She needs encouragement/confirmation ("Everything done correctly", "Almost done"), as it boosts her self-confidence.

## Wishes

- A application process that does not overwhelm them (small steps, clear structure).
- Reassurance and guidance at the beginning (overview, duration, next steps).
- Confidence that they are "doing everything right" – with encouragement/confirmation.
- Fewer mistakes and thus less follow-up requests/stress.
- Specific instructions for documents per section (what exactly, which time period).
- Assistance with unclear/old proofs (e.g., pension notice from earlier).
- A mode of "filling out together" without losing control.
- Understanding deadlines (monthly logic / preserving deadlines) and acting in time.
- Opportunity for dialogue (ask questions, chat/redirect to expertise).
- Transparency after submission (confirmation/status).

## Fears & Uncertainties

- Fear of entering something incorrectly and thereby facing disadvantages/delays.
- Fear of being overwhelmed (scope "not less than 80 pages").
- Uncertainty about which documents are really necessary (e.g., old pension notice).
- Concern about not being able to find/acquire documents.
- Uncertainty about deadlines and timeframes (and what "timely" means).
- Fear of additional claims and extra "paperwork."
- Fear that their application will not arrive or "disappear in the system."
- Hesitation to ask "silly questions" (needs a respectful tone).
- Concern about losing control when someone fills out forms "for them" (co-use must be well designed).

## Pain Points and Barriers

- Too many fields/irrelevant questions → cognitive overload.
- Unclear terms (e.g., hot water/heating) without examples.
- Document requirements are hard to understand (what exactly, how old, where from).
- Obtaining old/unclear proofs (e.g., contacting pension insurance).
- Lack of gap/error detection in the process (leads to follow-up requests).
- No or poor support for incomplete but deadline-compliant submissions.
- No clear status transparency after submission.
- Digital overload with apps/smartphones; a PC in the office is more realistic.
- Co-use is often necessary, but tools are often not designed for collaborative editing.
- Deadlines/monthly logic are critically important but often not explained in a user-friendly way.
- Stress/overwhelm due to cognitive limitations in complex decisions.

---

## Central Hypotheses

1. When the entry point is calming, clear, and expectations are set, the risk of drop-off for cognitively burdened users decreases.
2. When contextual logic filters out irrelevant information, understandability and completion rates increase.
3. When co-use is explicitly supported (mode, progress, re-entry), the quality of applications in the consulting setting improves.
4. When documents are announced in sections and specifically, requests for additional information and uncertainty decrease.
5. When missing documents are saved as to-dos (including "Request" assistance), users remain empowered to act instead of dropping out.
6. When "timely submission" is clearly explained, the proportion of applications submitted on time (for securing existence) increases.
7. When feedback is phrased supportively (not didactically), the willingness to correct increases.
8. When the final review explains risks clearly, critical gaps before submission are reduced.
9. When there is status transparency after submission, trust increases and support effort (inquiries) decreases.
10. When digital support and personal expertise are hybridly linked, more cases are successfully completed.

## User Stories

### User Story 1 – Entry & Reassurance

**As Sabine** (50–65) with cognitive limitations and fear of mistakes,
**I want** to receive a clear, reassuring entry with an overview,
**so that** I understand what to expect and am not immediately overwhelmed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Start screen explains in simple language: duration, steps, what I need.
- I see a clear sequence of steps (e.g., Situation → Housing → Income → Expenses → Documents → Submission).
- There is a note: "You can save at any time and continue later."

### User Story 2 – Context Logic (Yes/No) Hides Irrelevant Questions

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to see only relevant questions and have irrelevant questions automatically hidden,
**so that** I do not have to struggle through too many fields.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Questions are controlled by yes/no logic.
- Irrelevant sections do not appear (or are visibly marked as "not needed").
- Each question is briefly explained as to why it is being asked.

### User Story 3 – Supported Self-Service (Co-usage on PC)

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to be able to fill out the application together with a professional on the PC,
**so that** I receive support but still actively participate.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The flow works stably on PC/terminal.
- There is a visible mode "fill out with support" (without technical hurdles).
- The progress is clear so that we can directly resume after breaks.

### User Story 4 – Step-by-Step Blocks with Intermediate Successes

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to process the application in small, manageable sections,
**so that** I build confidence step by step.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Each section is short and has a clear title ("Your Apartment", "Your Insurances").
- After each section, I receive positive confirmation ("Section completed").
- I can save at any time and continue later without losing data.

### User Story 5 – Proactive Document Hints During Filling Out

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to see directly while filling out which documents belong to a section,
**so that** I can prepare and have less fear of follow-up requests.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- For each section, there is a document list (e.g., bank statements, rental documents, insurances).
- Hints are concrete ("last 3 months of bank statements") and in simple language.
- I can submit documents later and see them as a to-do.

### User Story 6 – Unclear/Old Evidence: Help & "Request" Option

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to receive help when I cannot find documents or do not know if they are needed,
**so that** I do not give up.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- For each document, there is: "I do not have this" / "Not available yet".
- The tool explains how/where I can request it (e.g., pension insurance).
- Open documents are automatically saved as to-dos.

### User Story 7 – Error and Gap Feedback with Solution Suggestions

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to receive clear feedback on what is missing or wrong – with concrete solutions,
**so that** I can correct it directly without feeling bad.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Missing fields/documents are clearly marked (What is missing? Why is it important?).
- There are concrete suggestions ("Here's how you can submit it...").
- The tone is supportive, not lecturing.

### User Story 8 – Final Check Before Submission (Quality Check)

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to see a final check before submitting,
**so that** I am sure that I have not forgotten anything important.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- An overview shows: complete / incomplete / unclear.
- I can jump directly to the places where something is missing.
- The final check explains the most important risks in simple language.

### User Story 9 – Timely Submission Despite Incompleteness

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to be able to submit the application even if incomplete in a timely manner,
**so that** I do not lose time and can still start receiving benefits on time.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- There is an option: "Submit now in a timely manner and add later".
- The tool briefly and clearly explains what "timely" means (month logic).
- After submission, I receive a to-do list for follow-ups including deadlines.

### User Story 10 – Deadlines, Timeframes, and Reminders

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to be informed and reminded in a timely manner about deadlines and timeframes,
**so that** I do not miss anything and can organize support.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Deadlines are visibly displayed at relevant points.
- I can activate reminders (e.g., email/SMS/print notice).
- The reminders refer to specific tasks ("Document X by date Y").

### User Story 11 – Confirmation of Receipt & Status Transparency

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to receive a clear confirmation and status after submission,
**so that** I have confidence that the application has been received and is being processed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- I receive a legally secure confirmation of receipt (download/print).
- Status is understandable (submitted → under review → follow-up request → decision).
- In case of follow-up requests: What is missing, by when, how to submit.

### User Story 12 – Dialogue Function + Referral to Expertise (Hybrid)

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to be able to ask questions and quickly reach a suitable counseling center if needed,
**so that** I do not get stuck and feel secure.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- A chat/question function is easily findable.
- There are clear options for personal help (hotline/appointment/counseling centers).
- I can return to the application without loss afterwards.

### User Story 13 – Clear Breakdown of Income and Expenses

**As Sabine,**
**I want** to record income and expenses in a clear, simple structure,
**so that** I do not get confused (rent, heating, hot water, insurances, assets).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The flow is structured by categories (income → housing → expenses).
- Terms are explained (e.g., hot water/heating) and examples are provided.
- Only relevant fields are shown (context logic).

## Requirements for the Tool (Chronologically Along the Process)

### 6.1 Entry: Simplification & Clear Logic

- Simplification of the complex, permanent disability situation in the application process.
- Context logic: clear yes/no logic that hides irrelevant questions and immediately explains which documents are needed for her situation (e.g., pension notice, housing cost breakdown).
- Only show the fields/requests that she really needs to fill out (no overwhelm).

### 6.2 Proactive Help When Filling Out (Cross-references, Examples)

- Proactive assistant that provides cross-references to required forms/documents during the application process.
- Contextual hints: attach last three months of bank statements; if children were present, highlight maintenance, child benefits, etc. (currently not relevant for Sabine, thus only as a logic example).

### 6.3 Documents & Evidence: Preparation, Finding, Submitting

- Hints about documents and forms so that she can prepare and gain more security/trust.
- Support with unclear or hard-to-obtain evidence:
  - Sabine does not know if she needs to submit the initial pension notice (if it is e.g., 20 years old).
  - She cannot find or obtain documents and needs help on how/where to request them and how to submit them.

### 6.4 Quality Check & Error Help

- Function that indicates what is wrong or forgotten – and directly offers solutions.
- Quality check/final control: highlight missing documents but still allow for timely submission ("You can submit now and add later").

### 6.5 Deadlines, Timeframes & Timely Submission (Critical)

- Sabine does not know submission deadlines and timeframes and needs understandable information about them in a timely manner.
- The logic of "timely submission" is important: If she submits on 27.01 (even if incomplete), it may be relevant for the claim month; submission on 01.02 may not be relevant anymore. (Example for existence security/month logic)
- She needs support to organize help in time (counseling, documents, follow-ups).
- This "month logic" is a central moment: The tool should explicitly explain what "timely" means and what she should submit immediately, even if documents are still missing.

### 6.6 Dialogue & Personal Expertise

- A chat function would help her ask questions because she likes to engage in dialogue.
- At the same time, it is important to her that a person with expertise is by her side (e.g., social psychiatric services, integration assistance/supported living, homeless assistance, social welfare offices, support associations, senior advisory services).
- She wants to be able to combine digital support and personal counseling.

### 6.7 Income/Expense Breakdown (Understandability)

- She needs a clear breakdown of income and expenses, e.g.: assets, insurances, rent, heating, hot water, etc.
- The structure should be understandable and guide her through the relevant points.

- What formats for reminders (email/SMS/print) fit the counseling setting and target audience?
- What status models after submission build trust and reduce follow-up questions?
- What contents should be prioritized in a final review (critical vs. nice-to-have)?
