## Biography (Short)

Sandor lives as a man with asylum rights (50–65 or 65+) alone in Berlin and faces a double hurdle in his daily life: tight finances and high housing costs, along with language and system barriers. He has no assets and cannot make ends meet with his available income; at the same time, it is difficult for him to understand which benefits and which path in the system are right for him. Mistakes can easily occur with housing costs (utilities/heating/hot water), or documentation may be missing — resulting in incomplete applications and delays in processing. Sandor often seeks support in a consulting setting (terminal/PC), but still wants to understand what he needs to provide and why. He primarily needs multilingual support, simple language, clear next steps, upload feedback, and the ability to submit on time and then follow up in an organized manner.

## Core Persona Profile

### Legal Framework & Benefit Logic

- Sandor operates within a complex system of residence law, social law, and administrative logics.
- He faces combined challenges (language, residence status, housing costs issues, possibly pension/disability law).
- For basic security, it is relevant that eligible persons have their "usual residence in the country."
- Core Benefit Logic: Eligibility depends on "usual residence in the country"; complex classification between systems (Asylum Benefits Act / Social Code II / Social Code XII) is often opaque for those affected.
- In Berlin, claims for benefits for foreign nationals refer to the Asylum Seekers Benefits Act as well as SGB II and SGB XII – the classification (which system applies) is often not transparent for those affected.
- § 42 SGB I: If the claim exists in principle and the determination of the amount is expected to take longer, an advance payment can/must be made (upon request); the advance payment begins no later than one month after the application is received.

### Income, Needs, and Financial Situation

- No assets.
- Before applying for basic security, Sandor received housing benefits.
- Gross need: household need exceeds €1,000 (especially due to high rent and heating costs).
- Net entitlement: approx. €790–900 (rough range).
- Duration of benefit receipt: over 4 years.
- For the application, income and asset proofs are typically required in Berlin (even with "no assets") to substantiate this.

### Living Situation & Housing Costs (KdU)

- Housing costs: above-average recognized KdU, especially in urban and "gentrification districts" (Mitte, Neukölln).
- Sandor has additional difficulties with detailed housing/utility cost proofs (e.g., utility bill, hot water regulation).
- Basic security includes, among other things, standard needs as well as costs for accommodation and heating; additional needs may be relevant in cases of decentralized hot water supply (often not understood or forgotten by those affected).
- In Berlin, application forms and attachments are provided for basic security/social assistance – including an attachment specifically for foreigners/asylum seekers (Attachment 2).

### Language, Comprehension, and Process Barriers

- He has difficulties with forms, required proofs, and bureaucratic language.
- Additionally, strong problems with technical vs. everyday language (terms around KdU, notices, proofs).
- Shame, fear of mistakes, and stress are significant hurdles.
- He often submits incomplete applications, leading to inquiries and delays.
- He does not understand why processing times are so long and wants to be informed about every step (in simple language, ideally with translation support).
- There is a lack of overview of the complex benefits landscape; he needs step-by-step guidance with expertise.

### Usage Context & Supported Self-Service

- Sandor often does not use the tool alone: In the office/reception, it can be quickly clarified what is missing, so he can possibly submit digitally immediately.
- He seeks counseling centers (Caritas, ASB, etc.) as an anchor point for "customer portal," where advisors can take a look with consent.
- Strong need for "supported self-service": terminals in offices/counseling facilities, help modules, and optional human support in the background.
- Some refugees additionally use their phones, research in chat groups, and exchange experiences (in addition to official sources).

## Wishes

- Immediately understand which benefits are applicable to him (so he doesn't "start off wrong").
- A start that reduces stress and provides a clear path.
- Translation/language support and simple language for all critical content.
- Only see relevant questions (no "empty fields", no confusion).
- Know very early: What documents do I need – specifically for my situation – and why?
- Step-by-step guidance on housing costs (rent → additional costs → heating → hot water).
- Be able to submit incomplete applications on time to avoid losing any time.
- Status transparency at every step (submitted/received/in review/open/decision).
- Process the application with support (advisory center/terminal) and submit it directly afterward.
- Shame- and anxiety-reducing UX (supportive, not judgmental).

## Fears & Uncertainties

- Fear of choosing the wrong application/pathway and losing time as a result.
- Fear of making mistakes (language, bureaucracy, incomprehensible terms) leading to delays/rejections.
- Shame for not understanding things / being unable to provide documents correctly.
- Stress from long processing times that he cannot comprehend.
- Uncertainty about what evidence is still required "with no assets" and why.
- Uncertainty regarding housing costs/water heating issues (rules seem technical and easy to forget).
- Concern about submitting incomplete information and getting caught in a loop of follow-up questions.
- Uncertainty about how advances/deadlines work (e.g., §42 SGB I – "when does payment start?").
- Fear of giving up without support because he cannot handle the complexity alone.

## Pain Points and Barriers

- Opaque system allocation (Asylum Benefits Act/Social Code II/Social Code XII) – affected individuals do not know "where" they belong.
- Language and comprehension barriers with forms, documentation, and bureaucratic language.
- Significant friction between technical administrative language and everyday understanding (housing costs, notices, documentation).
- Frequently incomplete applications → inquiries → delays.
- Housing cost documentation is detailed (itemized utility costs, hot water regulations) and prone to errors.
- Lack of overview: "What documents do I need exactly – and why?"
- High stress due to lack of explanation of deadlines/timeframes and their significance.
- Solo use is often unrealistic; without co-use/terminal concept, there is a risk of dropout.
- Multilingualism is often lacking precisely where it becomes critical (housing costs, documentation logic, status/next steps).
- Inconsistent district forms/attachments → uncertainty about "what is currently valid".

---

## Central Hypotheses

1. When the entry provides performance orientation (few questions → clear recommendation), the rate of incorrect application starts and consultation effort decreases.
2. When multilingualism + simple language are available at all times, data quality and completion rate increase.
3. When contextual logic filters out irrelevant information and explains "Why are we asking this?", confusion, shame, and dropouts decrease.
4. When documents are managed as a to-do list per section, incomplete submissions and follow-up requests decrease.
5. When upload quality checks (readability/completeness) are integrated, it reduces delays due to formal deficiencies.
6. When housing costs are designed as a guided process, inquiries about hot water/ancillary costs decrease significantly.
7. When timely submission and follow-up logic are standardized, the proportion of applications submitted on time despite gaps increases.
8. When status/monitoring is multilingual and simple, trust increases and inquiries decrease ("What is the status?").
9. When "supported self-service" works robustly in the consulting setting (PC/Web-first), dropouts and errors in complex cases decrease.
10. When information on foreign certificates (translation/periods/mandatory information) is integrated, the rejection/request rate decreases.

## Behavior & Usage Patterns (Digital Affinity & Tool Usage)

- Advisory centers (Caritas, ASB, etc.) serve as a portal/customer portal anchor point for him: advisors should gain insight with consent.

- He has a pattern of "submitting incomplete" → the tool must proactively manage (checklists/review/upload feedback).

## User Stories

### User Story 1 – Entry & Benefit Orientation

**As Sandor** (entitled to asylum, non-German, 50+),
**I want** to understand at the beginning which benefit(s) I am eligible for and what the right path is,
**so that** I do not start the wrong application and do not waste time.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The entry asks few, simple questions (e.g., residence/status, place of residence, household size).
- I receive a clear recommendation: "This is the right way" + "Why" (in simple language).
- I immediately see the next steps (e.g., "Which documents first").

### User Story 2 – Multilingualism & Simple Language

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to use the application in my language and understand all important content in simple language,
**so that** I can answer questions correctly and have less fear of making mistakes.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Language selection is always available (even in the middle of the application).
- Difficult terms have "What does this mean?" help in simple language.
- Optional: Read-aloud function / step-by-step mode (short sections, clear sentences).

### User Story 3 – Context Logic (Yes/No) Excludes Irrelevant

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to see only questions that are relevant to my situation,
**so that** I am not confused by empty fields and can get through faster.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Yes/No logic automatically excludes irrelevant questions/sections.
- The system briefly explains why a question is asked.
- There are no duplicate inquiries (already entered information is retained).

### User Story 4 – Supported Self-Service (Not Alone)

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to be able to process the application with support (e.g., in a counseling center at a PC/terminal),
**so that** I do not give up in case of uncertainty and can submit additional documents directly.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The process works stably on PC/Web (suitable for counseling/terminal).
- Saving & continuing later is possible at any time.
- Submitting documents is quickly findable and possible without restarting.

### User Story 5 – KdU/Housing Costs as a Guided Process

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to enter rent, utility costs, heating, and hot water in a guided manner,
**so that** I do not forget any details and fewer inquiries arise.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Housing costs are queried step by step (rent → utility costs → heating → hot water).
- For each step, I see which proofs are required (e.g., rental agreement, utility bill).
- Terms like "hot water regulation" are explained understandably (with examples/icons).

### User Story 6 – Immediate Document Hints Relevant to My Situation

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to see directly which documents I need exactly in my situation,
**so that** I can prepare and do not submit incomplete applications.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- For each section, there is a checklist ("What is needed here?").
- For each document, it briefly states: "Why is this needed?"
- The list can be used as a to-do (open/in progress/uploaded).

### User Story 7 – Example Documents & Format Aids

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to see examples of how documents should look (content/format),
**so that** I know what is accepted and what is missing.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- For central proofs, there are example views (anonymized) or templates.
- The tool shows: "Must include: period, amount, address, name…"
- Hints for common errors (e.g., wrong period, missing page, unclear amounts).

### User Story 8 – Upload with Quality Check (Readability & Completeness)

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to receive immediate feedback when uploading whether a document is readable and complete,
**so that** I can correct it directly and avoid delays.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The system warns of illegibility, wrong format, or missing pages.
- There are concrete solutions ("re-photograph", "as PDF", "page 2 is missing").
- Uploaded documents are clearly assigned to a section.

### User Story 9 – Foreign Proofs: Error Hints & Support

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to receive special hints when proofs from abroad might be problematic,
**so that** I can avoid inquiries and rejections.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Hints regarding language/translation, unclear periods, missing mandatory information.
- The tool indicates what exactly is missing and what alternative is possible (e.g., "additional proof").
- Marking in the final review, so I see it before submission.

### User Story 10 – Final Review Before Submission

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to see a clear final review before submitting,
**so that** I am sure that I have not forgotten anything important.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Overview shows: complete / incomplete / unclear.
- Missing points are clickable ("jump to the point").
- The tone is supportive ("Here's how to solve this…"), not discouraging.

### User Story 11 – Deadline-Compliant Submission Despite Gaps

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to be able to submit even incomplete applications on time,
**so that** I do not waste time and can submit additional documents later in a structured manner.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- There is an option "submit on time & add later."
- The tool briefly explains what this means (in simple language).
- After submission, a to-do list for follow-ups including deadlines is automatically created.

### User Story 12 – Status & Monitoring (Transparency Instead of Silence)

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to see the status of my application and the next steps at any time,
**so that** I understand why it is taking time and what I need to do.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Status levels are clear: submitted → received → under review → additional information requested → decision.
- Each status level explains: "What is happening right now?" + "What can I do?"
- In case of additional information requested: What is missing, by when, how to submit (directly from the status).

### User Story 13 – Deadlines & Reminders

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to be reminded in time about deadlines and follow-ups,
**so that** I do not miss anything and my process is not delayed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Reminders can be activated (e.g., push/email/SMS – depending on the channel).
- Reminders refer to specific tasks ("Document X by date Y").
- An overview shows all open deadlines in one place.

### User Story 14 – Ask Questions (Dialogue) Without Leaving the Process

**As Sandor,**
**I want** to be able to ask questions during the process (e.g., via chat/FAQ),
**so that** I can clarify uncertainties without giving up.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Help is always accessible ("Help" button per section).
- Answers are in simple language and preferably multilingual.
- I can then continue exactly at that point without losing anything.

## Sandor's Core Risk

Sandor's biggest risk is a **false start** (complex system landscape, lack of transparency). The entry must quickly guide him onto the right path and reduce stress.

## Requirements for the Tool (Chronologically Along the Process)

### 7.1 Entry: Orientation "What Benefits Can I Apply For?"

- At the beginning, Sandor needs a comprehensible overview: What benefits are generally possible and what fits his situation?
- The entry should reduce stress and provide a clear route.

### 7.2 Context Logic: Only Relevant Questions + Immediate Hints

- Structured inquiry of complex income and residence circumstances.
- Context-dependent hints: "What documents do you need – exactly in your situation?" (e.g., rental agreement, utility costs, proof of residence status).
- The tool should not only name documents but also indicate which fields become relevant and what to do next.

### 7.3 Prepare Documents (With Examples and Format Aids)

- Very early information on what proofs are needed and why.
- Example documents with appropriate content/format for reference (guidance).
- Error hints for typical problems (e.g., incomplete utility costs, unclear hot water specifications).
- The Berlin requirement list typically includes valid personal documents, possibly proof of residence, income and asset proofs, and (if relevant) proofs of full disability.

### 7.4 Incomplete, Deadline-Compliant Submission + Follow-Up

- Support for deadline-compliant submission, even if documents are still missing.
- Subsequently, a structured follow-up process: checklist, reminders, submission logic – visible for counseling centers.
- Clear explanation of deadlines/timeframes and why they are crucial.

### 7.5 Status, Monitoring & Transparency (Trust)

- Clear status: submitted / legally received / under review / open inquiries / decision.
- Comprehensible step-by-step feedback (simply formulated, optionally multilingual).
- Hint logic: "What is the next sensible step to avoid delays?"

### 7.6 Multilingualism & Visual Comprehensibility

- Multilingual user interface.
- Clear icons/images for complex terms (KdU, notices, proofs).
- Optional "simultaneous" translation for better communication.

### 8) Tool and Process Goals (Merged)

- Fewer incomplete submissions → fewer inquiries → faster processing.
- Clarity about relevant fields, necessary documents, next steps, and deadlines.
- Supported self-service (counseling + terminal + portal access) instead of sole app usage.
- PC/Web-first in counseling settings, mobile use rather supplementary (status/reminders/upload in exceptional cases).
