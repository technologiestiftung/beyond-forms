## Biography (Short)

Helmut is an older retired man from Berlin (senior age), married and a father of two children. He and his wife live in a 3-room apartment with high rent, while their pension income is very low and barely enough to cover daily expenses. Helmut has little trust in digital administrative processes and quickly feels overwhelmed by bureaucratic language — especially when it is unclear which documents are truly needed. Therefore, he needs simple, very clear step-by-step guidance, usually with support (from relatives or counseling), preferably on a computer. He only feels secure when he receives a clear confirmation, straightforward status updates, and understandable explanations of what will happen next.

## Core Persona Profile (Current Situation)

### Income, Savings, and Financial Situation

- Helmut has paid into the German pension insurance for over 17 years.
- Both were self-employed and have paid the minimum amount into the statutory pension insurance for over 30 years.
- Helmut's pension: €450 net.
- Wife's pension: €320 net.
- The wife works despite receiving a pension and has a small additional income.
- No assets; no housing benefit; living expenses not secured; receiving benefits for over 5 years.
- Gross need: €800–1,250 with a net entitlement of about €650–710 net per month.
- The information on "pension periods/contributions" (17 years vs. 30+ years of minimum contributions) and "countable income" appears somewhat inconsistent. For the persona, this can be seen as "complex/unclear for those affected" or marked internally as "numbers still to be reconciled."

### Living Situation and Fixed Costs

- Residing in Berlin, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf / Steglitz-Zehlendorf / Tempelhof-Schöneberg.
- 3-room apartment.
- Warm rent: €1,200 (cold rent not specified).
- High recognized housing costs, especially in western city center districts.
- Insurance (liability, household, and life insurance) together: €290.

### Reason and Goal (Why Basic Security?)

- Helmut and his wife want to apply for basic security because their ongoing income does not cover their total needs.
- They want to understand, process, obtain/submit the necessary documents for the application independently and submit it completely.
- At the same time, they have fear, uncertainty, and little overview of what requirements, documents, forms, and information are necessary to apply for basic security.

### Digital Competence and Usage Context

- Both have little media competence.
- Complex issues are often not understood, especially due to bureaucratic language, foreign words, academic language, and bureaucratic processes.
- For digital services (administration, health insurance, bank, etc.), they often need support from relatives or counseling centers (e.g., Caritas, district office).
- They are highly likely to use the tool not alone but together with support on the PC.
- Using a smartphone to complete services is the exception; it would be risky to offer the application only as a mobile app.
- Typical usage situation: quiet environment with support, rather step by step with interim successes (e.g., "You have completed the pension section, now it's about your apartment") instead of "everything in one go."

## Wishes

- Understand the application independently (without the "bureaucratic jungle").
- Step-by-step guidance instead of "everything at once."
- A clear answer: What comes next? What do I need to do?
- See intermediate successes to stay motivated.
- Only receive truly relevant questions (no overwhelm).
- Easily find, upload, and review documents – with feedback.
- Assurance that the application has been legally received (confirmation/download).
- Reminders for deadlines/appointments so nothing goes wrong.
- Work hybrid: start digitally and use personal assistance when needed.
- Trust that sensitive data is protected and how it will be used.

## Fears & Insecurities

- Fear of making mistakes and risking disadvantages or delays.
- Uncertainty about which requirements/documents/forms are truly necessary.
- Overwhelmed by bureaucratic language, jargon, and red tape.
- Distrust of digitalization: concern for sensitive data.
- Unclear that the process is multi-step (appointments, follow-ups, deadlines).
- Stress when documents are missing/unclear (e.g., old pension statements).
- Fear of "abandonment" when there is no help or something is unclear.
- Uncertainty regarding housing/cost of living issues (high rent, what will be recognized?).
- Worry that the application is "lost" if something goes wrong digitally (missing confirmation).
- Uncertainty due to inconsistent information on pension periods/income (complexity that the tool must address).

## Pain Points and Barriers

- Low media literacy (digital administrative processes are rarely manageable alone).
- Mobile-only would be risky (smartphones are rarely used for government processes).
- Bureaucratic language hinders understanding and correct entries.
- High demand for information and advice (pension, housing, proofs, deadlines).
- Fear of making mistakes → leads to avoidance/discontinuation.
- Documents are hard to find/acquire (old, unclear, multiple sources).
- Unclear process flow (multiple steps/appointments/additional submissions are not transparent).
- Lack of feedback (what is missing, what is illegible, what happens next).
- Missing trust anchors (data protection + legally secure confirmation of receipt).
- Co-use is often necessary, but typical tools are not designed for role logic and "filling out together."
- Inconsistencies (age, pension periods) show that users often perceive data as "unclear" → the tool must robustly address ambiguities (hints/options/referrals).

## Central Hypotheses

1. When the entry and step overview are very simple, dropout rates decrease among users with low media literacy.
2. When the tool uses contextual logic (only relevant questions), completion rates and data quality improve.
3. When intermediate successes are visible, Helmut stays motivated and continues to work in blocks.
4. When documents are managed section by section and checked for readability, follow-up requests decrease.
5. When the "multi-step process" is explained, it reduces uncertainty (deadlines/follow-ups/terms).
6. When there is a legally secure confirmation and status, trust increases and inquiries decrease.
7. When data protection is explained in everyday language situationally, willingness to apply digitally increases.
8. When co-use (role logic) is provided, the reality of "support at the PC" works better.
9. Proactive hints about additional benefits increase perceived usefulness and trust.

## Behavior & Usage Patterns (Digital Affinity & Tool Usage)

- The role works under time pressure and with many parallel cases – without an immediate overview, deadlines, submissions, and priorities are lost.
- Works PC/Web-first in a consulting setting (terminal/office), often under time pressure.
- Typical: PC usage in a quiet environment, step-by-step, with breaks.
- Smartphone is an exception for administrative/banking/cash-related topics.
- High acceptance when the tool shows intermediate successes and motivates.
- Requires a clear, logical structure: first pension, then other income/expenses only if necessary.
- Needs interim successes and confirmation to stay engaged.
- Needs status transparency to maintain trust in administration/process.
- In case of uncertainty, a visible path to hotline/appointments/advisory services is helpful.
- Distrust towards digital decreases when data protection is explained concretely and situationally.

## User Stories

### User Story 1 – Entry & Orientation

**As Helmut** (68), retiree with little media competence,
**I want** to receive clear, simple orientation at the start of the basic security application,
**so that** I understand what to expect and how the process works.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The start explains in simple language: purpose, duration, required documents, next steps.
- There is a clear step list (e.g., pension → housing → expenses → documents → submission).
- I can see at any time: Where am I right now? What comes next?

### User Story 2 – Step-by-Step Guidance with Interim Successes

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to be guided step by step and see interim successes,
**so that** I am not overwhelmed and stay motivated to complete the application.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Each section is a clear block with an understandable title (e.g., "Your Pension", "Your Apartment").
- After each block, I see an interim status ("section completed").
- I can pause sections and continue later without losing data.

### User Story 3 – Relevant Questions Instead of Overwhelm

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to answer only the questions that are truly relevant to my case,
**so that** I do not have to struggle through unnecessary content.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The tool asks context-dependent questions (detailed questions are only shown when relevant).
- There are no duplicate questions; already entered information is not asked again.
- Unclear terms have "What does that mean?" help in everyday language.

### User Story 4 – Understandable Language and Better Formulations

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to read questions and hints in very simple, everyday language,
**so that** I understand them without bureaucratic jargon and can answer correctly.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Formulations avoid technical terms ("granted", "countable", etc.) or explain them immediately.
- Examples help with understanding (e.g., "Warm rent = rent including additional costs").
- Texts are short, clear, and contain concrete calls to action.

### User Story 5 – Finding, Uploading, and Checking Documents

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to know what documents I need, easily find them, upload them, and have them checked,
**so that** I do not receive follow-up requests and my application is not delayed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- There is a document checklist per section (e.g., pension/housing/insurance).
- Upload accepts readable formats (PDF/JPG/PNG) and checks readability (note in case of problems).
- The tool clearly shows: What is still missing? What is illegible? What needs to be supplemented?
- There are direct links/hints on where to obtain documents.

### User Story 6 – "Not Yet Available" Option for Difficult Proofs

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to be able to indicate when a proof is not yet available,
**so that** I can still proceed and know how to request it.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- For each proof, there is the option "Not yet available – I will request it."
- The tool explains how/where I can request the proof (e.g., pension insurance, landlord).
- The tool keeps track of open proofs and displays them as a to-do list.

### User Story 7 – Deadlines & Reminders

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to be reminded of deadlines and appointments,
**so that** I do not miss anything and my application does not fail or get delayed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The tool clearly points out deadlines and explains what happens if I miss them.
- I can activate reminders (e.g., email/SMS/print notice).
- Open tasks are prominently displayed ("Only 3 days until deadline X").

### User Story 8 – Status & Legally Secure Receipt Confirmation

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to see the status of my application at any time and receive a confirmation,
**so that** I have confidence that my application has arrived and is being processed.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Status display: "submitted", "legally received", "under review", "follow-up request", "decision".
- After submission, I receive a download/print confirmation.
- In case of follow-up requests, it is clear: What is missing? By when? How do I submit it?

### User Story 9 – Hints on Additional Benefits (Trust & Benefit)

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to receive proactive hints about which costs can possibly be covered,
**so that** I do not miss opportunities and feel more secure.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Relevant hints appear contextually (e.g., for insurances: "can often be covered").
- Hints are in simple language and explain why this is relevant.
- There is an option "More about this" without interrupting the process.

### User Story 10 – Hybrid: Digital Path + Personal Consultation

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to be able to use personal consultation at any time,
**so that** I do not abandon the process in case of uncertainty.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Visible entry to hotline/appointment/counseling centers (e.g., Caritas/district office).
- I can continue the digital processing even if I use consultation in between.
- Co-use is supported (with relatives/counseling center on PC).

### User Story 11 – Transparent Data Protection & Data Use

**As Helmut,**
**I want** to understand how my sensitive data is protected and for what it is used,
**so that** I feel confident to submit the application digitally.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Clear data protection notice in simple language: What data? For what purpose? Who sees it?
- Hints appear exactly where sensitive data is requested.
- There is a short, understandable summary + optional details.

## Requirements for the Application Tool (Chronologically Along the Process)

### 7.1 Entry & Orientation

- The tool should proactively guide and clearly explain what happens next (even if forms need to be filled out or documents submitted).
- It should be designed in simple language, accessible operation, good help texts (e.g., rather "Have you applied for benefits?" instead of "Have you been granted benefits?") and support from relatives or counseling centers (Caritas, district office, relatives).
- The language should be as understandable as possible, regardless of language and culture.

### 7.2 Preparation Before the Application (Checklist & Clarity)

- Step-by-step guide on what information/documents need to be collected and prepared before submission to avoid delays.
- Interim successes should be visible (e.g., "You have completed the pension section – now it's about your apartment.").

### 7.3 Data Collection in the Application (Logical Structure, No Overwhelm)

- A clear "Pension" block with logical hierarchy: pension as main income, then further income/expenses only if needed and asked understandably (e.g., hot water via instantaneous water heater, alimony, capital gains, rental income, etc.).
- The application should only ask relevant questions based on the target group/status/age/profession, so that usage remains understandable and user-friendly.
- Avoid duplications: Do not ask for the same information multiple times, but continue cleanly.

### 7.4 Finding, Uploading, Checking Documents (With Feedback)

- Relevant forms should be easily findable.
- Documents to be submitted should be able to be uploaded in readable formats.
- The tool should inform (step by step) what is missing, what is illegible, or what needs to be corrected/complemented – including direct links to suitable sources/places.

### 7.5 Help with Difficult Proofs (Follow-up & Options)

- Support for hard-to-obtain proofs (e.g., pension notice from 20 years ago): indication of how/where to request it.
- Option like "Not yet available – I will request it."

### 7.6 Process Transparency, Deadlines & Status

- Explanation that the application process can take place in several steps/appointments (follow-ups, inquiries, deadlines).
- Reminder function for appointments and deadlines.
- Status tracking (e.g., submitted, under review, follow-up, decision).
- Transparent confirmation: "Your application has been legally received," including download/print confirmation, as trust in administration strongly depends on this.

### 7.7 Additional Notes & Trust

- Proactive hints about additional benefits/options (e.g., household and liability insurance can often be covered) – this builds trust and reduces risk.

### 7.8 Hybrid: Digital + Personal Support

- Desire for personal consultation (hotline/appointment), so that they can either use only digital support or flexibly switch between digital and personal consultation.

### 7.9 Data Protection & Data Use (Building Trust)

- Transparent information on how sensitive data is protected.
- Clear explanation of what data is used for what purpose.
- Accessibility should be specifically considered: large font, clear contrasts, reading function/easy language mode, clear error messages without technical terms.
- "Fill out with support" mode: role logic (applicant + assisting person), so that work can be done together without losing control/overview.

## Product Requirements

- Simple language (everyday language, short sentences, no technical terms).
- Barrier-free operation (large font, clear contrasts, read-aloud/easy language mode).
- Orientation & expectation management at the start (purpose, duration, documents, steps).
- Decision reduction: only necessary detail questions when relevant.
- No duplications (do not ask for information multiple times, keep it clean).
- Document checklists per section (retirement/housing/insurance, etc.).
- Option for difficult proofs ("Not yet available - I will request it").
- Process transparency (clearly explain multiple steps/appointments/follow-ups).
- Status tracking including follow-up requests: What is missing? By when? How to submit?
- Co-usage capability (applicant + assisting person, without loss of control).
